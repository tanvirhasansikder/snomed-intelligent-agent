# Research protocol v3.0 (Study 2)

**Faithful under Missing Evidence? A Controlled Study of LLM Answers about the SNOMED CT Hierarchy**

* Written 2026-11-19, after the Study 2 pilot and **before any CSE499 or paper-stage run**. The pilot was used for operational checks only (Section 10); no pilot score is reported as a finding.
* Frozen under the Git tag `protocol-v3.0-frozen`. Any later change goes into "Amendments" with a date and reason.
* Study 1 (protocol v2.0, `docs/RESEARCH_PROTOCOL.md`, tag `protocol-v2.0-frozen`) is complete and unchanged. It is the preliminary, live-retrieval study; Study 2 is the main study.
* Context: CSE499 project, North South University; deadline 2026-12-03; journal paper afterwards.

## 1. Problem

LLM answers grounded in a knowledge graph are only as good as the evidence retrieved. Study 1 showed, on live BioPortal retrieval, that partial evidence is common and goes with more unsupported claims (17.5% of B1's claims when its evidence was insufficient vs 3.5% when sufficient). But in Study 1, incompleteness was observed, not controlled: it depended on which concepts the crawler happened to reach.

Study 2 uses a complete local SNOMED CT release, so incompleteness can be **set**. The same question is answered with all of its answer-relevant evidence and with half of it withheld, and every claim is labelled exactly against the release.

**What is known** (novelty audit, 2026-11-18; `docs/LITERATURE_SEARCH.md`):
* Removing answer-path triples from a knowledge graph lowers LLM answer **accuracy** (Xu et al., EMNLP 2024; Zhou et al., EACL 2026; Wang et al., MissDiag, preprint 2026). None of these measures unsupported claims, abstention or citations, and none tells the model what is missing.
* Insufficient context leads to hallucination more often than abstention (Joren et al., ICLR 2025), judged with an LLM autorater on text passages.
* Explicit completeness statements reduce over-confident negative answers in synthetic text (Min et al., CROWN-QA, preprint 2026).
* Text retrieval suits single-hop questions and graph retrieval multi-hop ones (Han et al., KDD 2026).
* Ontology-grounded retrieval reduces clinical hallucination when the graph is complete (Ali et al., JBI 2026).

**What is open.** To our knowledge, no study measures how **controlled, answer-relevant** evidence loss changes the **evidential faithfulness** of LLM answers about an ontology hierarchy, labelled deterministically against an exact reference; whether **retriever-generated coverage marks** change that behaviour; and what deterministic verification costs at each completeness level.

## 2. Contributions

These are claims to be tested, not results.

* **C1: faithfulness under controlled evidence loss.** Nested, seeded withholding of the IS_A links each answer needs, with a matched random-removal control, on an exact reference (SNOMED CT International Edition 2026-02-01).
* **C2: coverage marks generated by the retriever.** The same evidence with and without per-list completeness lines.
* **C3: the price of deterministic verification** at each completeness level.
* **C4: graph vs text retrieval over the same source,** with an exact answer key: question-aware graph retrieval against lexical and dense retrieval over the release's own concept cards.

## 3. Research questions and hypotheses

Directions are stated before any run. Every test is paired on identical items.

| ID | Question | Hypothesis / analysis |
| --- | --- | --- |
| RQ1 | How does withholding answer-relevant evidence change faithfulness? | **H1a (primary):** G-QA@50 has more items with an unsupported claim and more abstentions than G-QA. **H1b (primary):** on items where the control can be matched, G-QA@50 has more items with an unsupported claim than G-QA@50:random (removal of the same number of links the answer does not need). Descriptive: withheld-fact restatement, three-way outcomes by sufficiency, invented IDs. |
| RQ2 | Do coverage marks change behaviour under missing evidence? | **H2 (primary):** at 50%, removing the marks (G-QA@50:unmarked) raises items with an unsupported claim and T3 over-closure, and lowers abstention, relative to G-QA@50. Secondary: the same contrast with complete evidence (G-QA vs G-QA:unmarked). |
| RQ3 | What does deterministic verification cost? | That G-QA+V has no claim unsupported by its evidence holds by construction and is **not tested**. **H3:** G-QA+V's task score is lower than G-QA's at 50% (the price of strictness). Whether the loss is larger at 50% than at 0% is compared descriptively, from the two paired contrasts. Descriptive: regeneration rate, claims removed by reference label. |
| RQ4 | Does structure-aware graph retrieval beat card retrieval? | **H4 (primary):** G-QA scores higher than L-RAG and than D-RAG, mainly on tasks needing structure beyond one card (T2 child lists, T3 negatives, T6), and has fewer items with an unsupported claim. Per-task results are reported whatever their direction. |
| RQ5 | Do the effects hold on a second model? | Descriptive replication with Gemma 4 31B on the subset it completes (Section 6). |

H1a, H1b, H2 and H4 are primary. Holm correction is applied within each research question and model.

## 4. Data

* **Source:** `snomed.db` (Hugging Face `InteliLab/SNOMED_DB`), provided by the supervisor and confirmed as the project's final dataset on 2026-11-18. SQLite, 2.73 GB, SHA-256 `9b0d17fd348198ee1b2eaae7ee55e974e27fda7fb5868f55a94370622a1b6eeb`.
* **Release: SNOMED CT International Edition 2026-02-01,** verified on 2026-11-18 against tx.fhir.org: the newest International concepts present carry effectiveTime 2026-02-01, and IDs published 2026-03-01 or later are absent. It is not the newest monthly release, and the paper says "2026-02-01", not "latest".
* **Contents:** `concepts` (419,712, each with a BGE-M3 vector and a concept card), `concept_names` (737,678), `edges` (685,653 IS_A links), FTS5 indexes. There is no active flag, effectiveTime or relationship type; IS_A only.
* **Evaluation view: International.** The database also holds extension concepts (UK, US, Australian and others). Extension concepts are removed; International parents are bridged over removed concepts, then redundant links are removed by transitive reduction (`knowledge_graph/snomed_db_loader.py`). Result: 351,751 concepts, 574,430 IS_A links, 0 cycles, 7,775 concepts bridged, 10,195 redundant parents removed; manifest in `data/manifests/snomed_db_manifest.json`.
  * **Known defect:** 871560001 lacks its official parent 871555000 in the database, so it and its child 1269559008 are unreachable from the root and are excluded.
  * 17 of 19 top-level hierarchies are present (Special concept and SNOMED CT Model Component are absent).
* **Cross-check with Study 1's BioPortal reference** (root-adjusted): direct parents agree for 98.2% of concepts (603/614), sampled ancestor sets for 94.2% (113/120), child lists for 79.2%.
* **Concept cards and vectors.** Each card states the term, type, synonyms, parent names, one path to the top of its hierarchy, attributes, and children cut at about 12 ("+N more"); no IDs. The stored vectors were verified on 2026-11-19 to be BGE-M3 dense embeddings of `card_text` (cosine 1.0000 on 5 of 5 test concepts).
* **Sampling** (`evaluation/collect.py`, sampler `stratified-v3`): the six Study 1 hierarchies (Clinical finding, Procedure, Body structure, Substance, Pharmaceutical/biologic product, Organism) × three depth bins below the hierarchy's top concept (shallow 1–3, middle 4–6, deep 7+) × leaf / non-leaf.
  * **main:** 4 per stratum, 144 concepts, seed 2026111901;
  * **pilot:** 1 per stratum, capped at 2 per hierarchy, 12 concepts, seed 2026111902.
  * Study 1's concepts and the pilot are excluded from the main sample; the overlap is 0.
  * Both data sets were collected with code revision `2d467f4`; the seeded sample is identical at `691483a`, where the sampler was committed.
* **Data handling:** the database, collected data and results stay in Git-ignored folders and are never published. Experiment prompts contain SNOMED CT terms and are sent to Google's API; the supervisor approved free-tier use on 2026-11-19 (Section 11).

## 5. Questions

As Study 1 (protocol v2.0, Section 5), with gold answers from the International view. T2 asks for the children of the sampled concept's parent on its descent path. The main data set gives **979 questions**: T1 144, T2 125, T3 278 (143 yes, 135 no), T4 144, T5 144, T6 144.

Each item gets two deterministic evidence labels, `sufficient` and `answer_in_evidence`, as in Study 1, now computed on the evidence each condition actually shows.

## 6. Systems and conditions

| Code | System | Evidence |
| --- | --- | --- |
| R0 | Deterministic reference answer, no model | scored on every distinct evidence context (`R0|<condition>`) |
| B0 | Closed-book LLM | none |
| G-QA | LLM + question-aware graph retrieval (Study 1's S1) | the S1 planner on the local graph |
| G-QA+V | G-QA + deterministic verification and one regeneration (Study 1's S2) | as G-QA |
| L-RAG | LLM + lexical card retrieval | BM25 over the database's card index |
| D-RAG | LLM + dense card retrieval | cosine similarity over the stored BGE-M3 card vectors |
| G-UP | LLM + upward-only crawl (Study 1's B1) | paper stage only |

**Conditions** are named `<system>[@<percent withheld>][:random][:unmarked]` (`evaluation/study2_runner.py`).

**G-QA retrieval** reads the frozen database through `crawler/local_client.py`, which offers the same calls as Study 1's BioPortal client. The request budget is **1,000 per question**. Study 1 used 60 because BioPortal calls were slow; on the local graph, pilot questions needed a median of 6, a 95th percentile of 34 and a maximum of 69 requests, so 60 would have cut some retrievals short.

**Card retrieval** (`evaluation/text_retrieval.py`, version 3; `evaluation/dense_retrieval.py`):
* Queries are the names of the concepts the question is about: one for T1, T2 and T5; two for T3, T4 and T6, with the 10 cards shared 5 + 5 and interleaved by rank. A first version that searched with the whole question text matched instruction words to unrelated cards and was rejected before any model call (2026-11-18).
* Only cards of International concepts are returned. Each card is shown verbatim, plus two additions so that every system uses the same prompt and verifier: the concept ID after each name the card mentions (matched only among that concept's real parents, path and children, so exact), and an edge ID for each IS_A link the card states.
* Card evidence is restricted to the evaluation view: extension concepts named on a card keep their name but get no ID, and only links present in the International view become evidence edges. Found in the pilot, where a card's Australian-extension parent appeared as an unknown identifier even in R0.
* No coverage lines are added; the card's own "(+N more)" remains.

**Completeness mask** (`evaluation/completeness.py`, version 2):
* **Answer-relevant links per task:**
  * T1: the concept's links to its gold parents;
  * T2: the gold children's links to the concept;
  * T3 "yes" and T4: every link on an upward path to the target;
  * T3 "no": the concept's whole upward closure;
  * T5: the concept's own parent and child links;
  * T6: every link from either concept up to a gold lowest common ancestor.
* **Order:** a seeded shuffle (seed `2026111903:<item ID>`); level f withholds the first max(1, round-half-up(f × n)) links, none at f = 0. Levels are nested.
* Only links are removed. A concept is dropped only if it is left with no link; the question's concepts always stay. Every list that lost a link and was complete becomes `partial`.
* **Marked** evidence is the Study 1 format with coverage lines (now including PARTIAL); **unmarked** is the same numbered links with no coverage, frontier or budget lines (format `v2-nocov`). Sufficiency labels, R0 and the G-QA+V verifier always use the true coverage.
* **Random-removal control:** the same number of links, drawn from the evidence links the answer does not need. Items with too few such links are skipped (no model call) and listed. In the pilot it could be matched only for T3, T5 and T6, because T1, T2 and T4 evidence holds only the answer's own links.

**Prompt v3.0** (`llm/prompt_manager.py`): identical to Study 1's v2.0 except for one question. The claim schema has no relation for "X is not a kind of Y", so in Study 1 and in the first Study 2 pilot the model supported a "no" by writing the asked relation itself, a false claim that verification then removed (Study 1: 55 of S1's 64 false-claim items). T3 now says to support "no" with ancestor claims and never to claim the asked relation when answering "no". In the pilot rerun, G-QA+V answered all 3 certified "no" items correctly (0 of 3 under v2.0). v2.0 remains the default, so Study 1 is reproducible.

**Models:**
* **Primary:** `gemini-3.5-flash-lite` (Google Gemini API, OpenAI-compatible endpoint), as in Study 1: temperature 0, JSON mode, `reasoning_effort=low`, up to 8,192 output tokens. Overload retries wait 10 s, doubling up to 3 retries.
* **Second (RQ5):** `gemma-4-31b-it` through the same API, open weights. It accepts the prompt and JSON mode, but on 2026-11-19 only 3 of 10 smoke-test calls and 46 of 62 pilot calls succeeded (HTTP 503 overload), at 42–96 s per answer. It is therefore run only on the pilot questions, in the background, and reported on whatever subset it completes. Overload retries wait 15 s, doubling up to 6 retries (at most 120 s each).
* Every call records the model ID returned, settings, prompt version, tokens, latency, finish reason and raw output; every response is cached.
* **No repetitions** in the CSE499 stage, to fit the free quota. Study 1 measured agreement of repeated answers at temperature 0 (0.50–0.83 by system); this is carried over as a threat to validity.

## 7. Measures

All claim labels are Study 1's (`explanation/explainer.py`): supported by the evidence, true but not retrieved, false in the reference, contradicted, unknown identifier, insufficient evidence. All Study 1 metrics are reported (protocol v2.0, Section 7). Study 2 adds (`evaluation/study2_report.py`):

| Measure | Definition | RQ |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Items with an unsupported claim | share of answers with at least one claim not supported by the shown evidence (**primary outcome**) | 1, 2, 4 |
| Withheld-fact restatement | of the gold parents (T1) or children (T2) whose link was withheld, the share the model still names | 1 |
| Three-way outcome | correct / abstained / wrong, split by `sufficient` (after Joren et al., 2025) | 1–4 |
| Grounded refusal | precision, recall and F1 of abstention, with "evidence insufficient" as the positive class; exact labels (after Song et al., 2025) | 1, 2 |
| Over-closure | T3 answers "no" when the evidence cannot certify it | 2 |
| Under-closure | T3 "no" questions with certifying evidence not answered "no" | 2 |
| Invented IDs | claims with an unknown identifier; cited IDs with an invalid check digit | 1, 4 |
| Evidence size | characters of rendered evidence | 4 |

## 8. Analysis

* **Pre-specified paired contrasts** (`CONTRASTS` in `evaluation/study2_report.py`): G-QA vs G-QA@50; G-QA@50:random vs G-QA@50; G-QA vs G-QA@50:random; G-QA@50 vs G-QA@50:unmarked; G-QA vs G-QA:unmarked; G-QA@50 vs G-QA+V@50; G-QA vs G-QA+V; L-RAG vs G-QA; D-RAG vs G-QA; B0 vs G-QA.
* For each contrast: items with an unsupported claim, abstention and items with a false claim (exact McNemar); task score per task (McNemar for T3, T4 and T6; Wilcoxon signed-rank for T1, T2 and T5); T3 over-closure for RQ2; the paired bootstrap difference in the unsupported-claim rate.
* Holm correction within each research question and model; 95% percentile bootstrap CIs (1,000 resamples, seed 0) for every rate; effect sizes with every p-value.
* Results per model, never pooled; by task, hierarchy and depth bin; split by whether the answer stayed derivable through another path after masking (`answer_in_evidence`).
* **No result is dropped.** Failed or unparseable answers count as failures. Skipped random-control items are listed with their count.
* The analysis code is committed with this protocol and not changed after the first CSE499 call, except to fix a bug, which becomes an amendment.

## 11. Threats to validity and limitations

* **Data:**
  * one release (International 2026-02-01) in a third-party SQLite build, not an RF2 distribution; IS_A only, English terms;
  * the International view is a projection of a database that mixes editions; one known defect (871560001);
  * the reference is the same graph the systems retrieve from, so "true but not retrieved" means "in the release but not shown".
* **Design:**
  * the mask removes all answer-relevant links in a fixed way; real retrieval failures are messier. Study 1 provides the live counterpart;
  * withholding even one needed link usually makes the evidence insufficient (pilot: at 25%, 5 of 84 items stayed sufficient, all T4 via another path), so levels act as graded amounts of missing information rather than graded sufficiency;
  * the random control can be matched only for T3, T5 and T6;
  * text and graph evidence differ in size (pilot means: cards about 6,600–8,000 characters; marked graph evidence about 2,300–3,100); evidence size is reported, and the paper stage adds a size-matched text run;
  * template questions; one prompt per task; the question type is given to G-QA's planner; concept labels can reveal hierarchy (lexical cues), counted as unsupported where not in the evidence.
* **Models:**
  * nondeterminism at temperature 0 (Study 1) with no repetitions in the CSE499 stage;
  * hosted models change behind fixed names; mitigated by recorded responses;
  * the second model covers only a small subset because of provider availability; effects may not generalise across model families.
* **Provider and data:** free-tier prompts may be used by the provider to improve its products; approved by the supervisor for the CSE499 stage; the paper stage uses the paid tier, where prompts are not used this way.
* **Evaluation:** scoring relies on the model listing its claims (Study 1, Section 9); no clinician or terminologist was involved; no claim of clinical utility.

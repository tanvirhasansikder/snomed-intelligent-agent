**Current preference (updated 2026-11-03: no clinician involvement; to revisit after the pilot):**

Without a clinician or terminologist, the paper is a *methods and terminology-informatics* study: it reports automatic correctness measured against SNOMED CT, and makes no claims about clinical usefulness. This changes the ranking.

1. **Journal of Biomedical Semantics:** best topical fit for an ontology and terminology method with automatic evaluation, and does not depend on clinical evaluation. Check the current fee and any waiver on its submission page before choosing it.
2. **Journal of Biomedical Informatics:** no fee via the subscription route, but its stated expectation of healthcare-professional involvement raises the risk of desk rejection. If chosen, state this openly in the cover letter and limitations, and stress the generalisable methodological lesson.
3. **BMC Medical Informatics and Decision Making:** if the country-tiered fee (25% for Bangladesh) is confirmed for this journal.

JMIR Medical Informatics drops to a fallback: its US$2,750 fee is far above the project budget.

**Requirement for all of them:** a SNOMED CT licence covering the study (`docs/LICENSING.md`). Reviewers may ask how terminology licensing was handled.
